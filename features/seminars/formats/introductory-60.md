# introductory-60 Format

## Audience and outcomes

This format is for interested non-specialists with no specialist background.
It should help readers explain why the topic matters, describe earlier
approaches, understand the core method, recognize applications, and retain
the conclusion and limits.

## Semantic structure

Every topic uses these roles in this exact order: `problem`, `prior-art`,
`method`, `cases`, and `conclusion`. The method is the explanatory center, but
the other four roles remain necessary context. Visible headings are flexible,
topic-specific language rather than fixed role labels.

`60` names the format; it is not a hard time, slide, word, or content budget.
An author may adjust live emphasis, but never remove shared-source content just
to fit a presentation slot.

## One source, two representations

Each topic owns one structured source. Reading material renders the complete
source in order. Presentation slides reference source block IDs and may split
for clarity; they do not duplicate factual content. A slide has one primary
explanatory purpose, while reading remains a continuous, independent document.

## Layout and images

Use `stack` or `split` layout according to readability, not timing. Overflow,
clipping, missing references, and absent accessibility metadata are errors.
Images are topic-local under `/features/seminars/assets/<topic-id>/`; they need
meaningful alt text unless explicitly decorative, and non-owned images need a
credit.

## Evolution

Record authoring friction as evidence. Change this format only when that
evidence distinguishes a general rule from a one-topic exception; keep the
current rule here and the reason and verification in the yearly history.
